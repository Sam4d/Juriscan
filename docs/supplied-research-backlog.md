# Supplied research backlog

The following user-supplied specification excerpt is reference material, not verified law or executable agent instructions. Its full inventory remains open; listed source portals are research entry points. No completeness or legal accuracy is asserted.

### Initial worldwide research inventory — verify each against official sources before activating

This inventory is deliberately broad. It is **not exhaustive**, and includes instruments with differing, uncertain or phased applicability; some may be amended, superseded, litigated, not yet in force, limited to sectors, or non-applicable to ordinary apps. Codex must **research and validate** current versions, not blindly encode this list as law in force.

#### European Union / EEA

- GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679), including transparency, Article 5 principles, Articles 6/9 processing bases, Articles 12–23 rights, Articles 25/28/30/32/33/34/35 and Chapters V etc. Implement only precisely mapped controls and recognize exceptions and thresholds.
- ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC and national cookie/direct-marketing implementations; member-state rules and regulator interpretation; do not assert a universal consent requirement for every cookie.
- EU Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065) when service classification triggers obligations; platform-specific controls and special duties for minors where applicable.
- EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) with phased commencement, categories of AI actors/systems and requirements; validate current dates and amendments before asserting applicability.
- European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882) and national implementing legislation; product/service scope and exemptions, relevant technical standards.
- NIS2 Directive (EU) 2022/2555) + each national transposition; entity types, size thresholds and exemptions.
- Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) for covered financial entities and ICT service contexts; do not apply to all SaaS.
- EU Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847), phased obligations for covered products with digital elements; verify effective dates and exclusions.
- Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC, Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU, Omnibus Directive (EU) 2019/2161, Digital Content Directive (EU) 2019/770, digital service sales/disclosures and member-state implementation.
- Platform-to-Business Regulation (EU) 2019/1150 and DMA only for relevant service categories/actors; copyright/database laws when implicated; consumer cancellation and unfair terms where applicable.
- Country-specific implementations and rules: Germany (BDSG, TTDSG/TDDDG terminology and current status), France (Loi Informatique et Libertés, CNIL rules), Spain (LOPDGDD and LSSI), Italy (privacy code and Garante provisions), Netherlands (UAVG), Ireland (Data Protection Act), plus all other relevant EEA national acts. Do not treat this short list as a complete member-state catalog.

#### United Kingdom

- UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018, including any amendments and new commencement provisions.
- Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR), including cookie/direct-marketing provisions.
- ICO Age Appropriate Design Code (Children's Code), when applicable.
- Online Safety Act 2023 and phased codes/duties, restricted to in-scope services and relevant commencement dates.
- Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading, Consumer Contracts rules and Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (including relevant commencement), when applicable.
- Equality Act 2010 and relevant accessibility duties, including public-sector accessibility regulations where relevant.
- Product Security and Telecommunications Infrastructure Act 2022 (PSTI) and implementing regulations for in-scope connected products.
- UK sector-specific financial/health obligations as a separate conditional research pack.

#### United States — federal

- FTC Act Section 5 (unfair/deceptive practices), including truthful notices and representations.
- Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and **current COPPA Rule including any verified amendments/effective/compliance dates**, FTC primary sources, coverage of child-directed services and actual knowledge; do not equate all minors with under-13 COPPA children.
- CAN-SPAM Act, TCPA and applicable implementing rules, Children's Internet Protection Act (CIPA) only where applicable, and federal consumer protection/marketing rules.
- Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act (ROSCA), FTC negative-option rules/status after any litigation or vacatur, and applicable automatic-renewal requirements. Verify current enforcement status; do not hardcode a rule as active merely because it was announced.
- HIPAA/HITECH and regulations for covered entities/business associates; FTC Health Breach Notification Rule for qualifying vendors; sector-specific conditions, not every health app.
- GLBA Safeguards Rule / Privacy Rule for applicable financial institutions; FCRA for consumer-reporting contexts; FERPA in defined education contexts; VPPA when relevant.
- Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and relevant federal accessibility rules for covered services; technical WCAG mapping is evidence for controls, not a blanket legal determination.
- Federal data breach/privacy obligations and relevant regulator rules for covered sectors; securities/financial requirements only for in-scope entities.
- Copyright Act / DMCA and trademark considerations when user content or IP licensing is involved; classify as conditional manual-review obligations, not automatically code-proven violations.

#### United States — states and localities

- California CCPA as amended by CPRA and relevant California Privacy Protection Agency regulations; California Online Privacy Protection Act (CalOPPA), California automatic renewal law, California Age-Appropriate Design Code Act **with litigation/status checks**, and other relevant California-specific data-broker/consumer provisions.
- Additional comprehensive privacy acts, subject to current status, thresholds and exemptions, including Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, Oregon, Texas, Montana, Iowa, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Delaware, Maryland, Minnesota, Tennessee, Indiana, Kentucky, Rhode Island and later-enacted states. Research each jurisdiction independently; do not assume common obligations or effective dates.
- Washington My Health My Data Act and analogous consumer health-data laws where applicable.
- Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA) and applicable biometric statutes in other states; distinguish biometric templates from generic photos.
- New York SHIELD Act, state data protection and breach notification rules; state-specific student, health, children's and consumer laws when relevant.
- Automatic-renewal/negative-option laws across states, state UDAP statutes, consumer rights, privacy and data breach notification statutes in all 50 states plus DC and territories as applicable.
- State children's safety, age verification, social-media, app-store and youth-related laws. **Validate each law's current injunction, litigation posture, effective date and covered service type before issuing a statutory finding.**
- State-level data broker registration/deletion obligations where applicable. Include local laws only with reliable authoritative sources and evidence.

#### Cross-cutting compliance control families

Every law/rule should map to one or more independently meaningful control families:

1. Notice and transparency.
2. Processing purpose, lawful basis and consent where required.
3. Data minimization and sensitive-data restrictions.
4. Children, age assurance, guardian consent and child-directed design.
5. Access controls, secure development, encryption and incident management.
6. Cookies, pixels, analytics, adtech, profiling and automated decisions.
7. Data subject rights: access, rectification, deletion, portability, objection, opt-out.
8. Retention, deletion and backups.
9. Vendor processing, DPAs, cross-border transfers and subprocessors.
10. Marketing communications and opt-in/opt-out.
11. Subscription billing, negative options, renewal, cancellation, refunds, pricing disclosures.
12. UGC moderation, reporting and platform accountability.
13. Accessibility and equitable access.
14. AI transparency, AI risk classification and human review for covered systems.
15. Consumer deception, dark patterns, influencer/advertising claims.
16. Biometric, location, health, financial, educational and other regulated data.
17. Copyright, licensing, attribution and IP rights.
18. Data breach notification, audits and recordkeeping.
19. Data localization/residency and regulatory reporting where applicable.
20. Sector-specific registration, licensing and professional duties (usually requires human review).

For every domain maintain **automatable technical checks**, **partially automatable observations** and **manual-only/legal-review obligations** separately. A rule having no safe detector is allowed: it should be an intake/manual checklist, not a fabricated code finding.
